# Bonk Club

Development rules: [project constitution](AGENTS.md). Current work and next-chat
context: [handoff](docs/HANDOFF.md).

A browser physics brawler for 1–4 people. Original stick-figure artwork and arenas, inspired by the feel of party fighting games including Stick Fight: The Game. Not affiliated with Landfall.

## Play

Choose **Single player** to start immediately against three AI opponents. **Online multiplayer** creates a pregame lobby and immediately displays a six-character invite code, with copy-code and copy-link buttons. Friends join the lobby; the host presses **Start match** once, without ready checks. Friends can also join available player slots during play. Each device controls one player with keyboard, controller or touch. Solo has four fighters. In the online lobby, click a non-host slot to choose **AI/Player** (AI until someone joins), **AI only**, **Player only** (empty until someone joins), or **Closed**. Closing an occupied slot or making it AI only removes its guest. Slot choices lock when the match starts; hot joins still work in available AI/Player and Player only slots. At least two fighters are required to start. Departing players reset that slot's score; a Player only slot stays empty, while AI/Player returns to AI. Use **Settings** on the main menu or the host lobby settings to choose the map rotation and **Easy**, **Normal** or **Hard** AI. Easy is the default and the choice is saved on the host's device. Set your name, one of eight colours and one of six hairstyles in the lobby or **Character** menu. Choices are saved on your device, shown to other players, and carried across rounds. Human players reserve distinct colours; AI use the remaining colours. Changing appearance does not reset scores or combat state.

| Action                            | Keyboard / mouse | Standard controller |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------- |
| Move                              | A / D            | Left stick / D-pad  |
| Aim                               | Mouse            | Right stick         |
| Double jump                       | W / Space        | A / cross           |
| Punch / fire                      | Left mouse / E   | X / square or RT    |
| Parry with fists / alternate fire | Right mouse / G  | B / circle or LT    |
| Lie down                          | Hold S           | LB or D-pad down    |
| Throw weapon                      | F                | Y / triangle        |

Weapons are picked up automatically when an unarmed player walks close enough. Throw the held weapon to free the slot; a thrown weapon retains its ammunition and can knock a living opponent into a physical tumble. Heavier weapons deliver more force and a longer knockdown. Fighters recover control once they can get up into clear space, including beneath low ceilings and on moving platforms.

On phones, use **Online multiplayer** with one player per device. Single player supports the same touch controls against three AI opponents.

| Touch area    | Gesture                                                                                        |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Left          | Drag left/right to move; release to stop                                                       |
| Left          | Swipe up to jump, then swipe up again for the second jump                                      |
| Left          | Drag down and hold to lie down                                                                 |
| Right         | Drag to aim and fire, or hold to fire in the current direction                                 |
| Right         | Double-tap to throw the held weapon without firing first                                       |
| Action button | Tap to parry with fists, double shot with shotgun, charged shot with plasma; hidden for other weapons |

Portrait uses a camera that follows the local player, an arena overview, and a separate thumb-control area. Landscape shows the full arena with transparent thumb controls. Gestures reset on opening a menu, focus loss, cancellation and viewport changes. Touch menus respect phone cutouts and keep form controls large enough to use without zooming.

Escape opens or closes the game menu. The simulation continues while menus are open, while another tab is visible, and after focus loss. A dedicated worker clock drives simulation and network sends independently of rendering. Closing the browser or the operating system suspending/discarding the tab still stops its execution.

## Combat

- Active ragdolls with 11 independently simulated particles, 10 bone constraints, pose springs, inertia and platform contacts. Knockouts preserve the current body pose and velocity.
- Impulse combat, landing compression, flailing limbs, recoil and double jumps. With empty hands, press parry for a 160 ms window that stops one melee hit or reflects one bullet, then closes. The cooldown is 850 ms from activation, with a recovery bar under the fighter and a touch-button countdown. Release before pressing again: holding never repeats or guards. Holding any weapon, including a bat or sword, prevents parrying. Explosions cannot be parried.
- Jump input is buffered for 120 ms, including presses just before landing. Walking off a ledge allows a 90 ms ground-jump grace period, then leaves one air jump. Holding jump does not repeat it. Small head catches at a platform edge are corrected only if the adjacent space is clear. The second jump now has a 680-unit impulse; route landings generally span 180-240 units vertically, with ledges moved clear of overhangs. Outside city stairs have one intermediate landing between each 320-unit storey.
- Holding down makes a fighter lie flat and lowers their collision shape. Release to stand when there is headroom.
- Twenty-four arenas. The original eight now have distinct routes and themed scenery, including open courtyards, staggered terraces, side routes and moving-platform shafts. Interior stairs are staggered so each landing has a clear approach. The default rotation cycles through all maps before repeating. Choose skyscrapers only or a fixed map through Arenas on the main menu. Twelve new maps cover jungle canopies and temples, desert canyons and ruins, garden houses and a mansion, hospital wards and an atrium, an arctic station, a volcanic quarry, a cargo port and an abandoned factory. Each has a distinct layout, scenery, terrain materials and cover.
- Arenas span 2560 × 1440 world units, with the full arena visible in landscape. Fighters keep their physical size, so they appear half as tall at the same display size. Running is limited to 240 units/second; hit and recoil impulses can still exceed that speed.
- Tall skyscraper rooms with 320 units between main floors (previously 180), separate elevator shafts, outside climbing routes, bridges and longer sightlines. Elevators carry standing or prone players and loose weapons in both directions.
- Tables, crates, logs, rocks, sofas, hospital beds, cabinets and barrels block movement, melee and projectiles. Damage cracks cover, then breaks it into simulated fragments. Concrete floors stop shots and explosions between storeys.
- Weapon damage and knockback have been retuned: bats hit for 55, swords 42, pistols 24, rockets 100 and grenades 95 before distance/cover effects. Bullets, fire and ice gain up to 30% damage at close range; shotgun pellets gain up to 45%. Bonuses decrease with travelled distance, including bounces, and never reduce base long-range damage. Larger ordinary explosion rings show their blast reach; firing sounds and hit flashes are stronger.
- 23 weapons: bat, sword, pistol, shotgun, rocket launcher, grenade, minigun, railgun, plasma cannon, triple rocket launcher, SMG, burst rifle, flamethrower, freeze ray, ricochet gun, sawblade launcher, Tesla gun, homing launcher, cluster launcher, heavy machine gun, repulsor, black hole generator and nuclear grenade. Fire burns after contact; ice locks a fighter in a frozen pose for 1.15 seconds, with a thaw window before another freeze; saws pierce and bounce; lightning chains across nearby opponents with a clear line of sight; homing rockets turn toward visible targets; cluster grenades scatter six smaller bombs; repulsors launch fighters and redirect shots; black holes pull in everyone and loose items, including their owner. The heavy machine gun only fires while prone on a floor.
- Each round opens with two featured rare/exotic pickups, rotating through that arsenal across rounds. Round 1 and every third round after it feature a nuclear grenade. Other pickups use 40% common, 32% uncommon, 20% rare and 8% exotic weighting before duplicate avoidance; the nuke has double weight inside the exotic tier (4% when all weapons are available). Non-featured spawns avoid types already on the map when possible. Opening locations are shuffled so a particular slot does not always receive the exotic weapon. Pickups prefer clear space beside cover; later drops favour landings near living fighters and avoid existing piles. Further weapons arrive every 3–5 seconds. Rare and exotic pickups have blue and purple glows respectively.
- Holding attack while unarmed chains punch (25 damage), kick (32), and spinning finisher (43). The first two strikes keep opponents close; the finisher hits around the fighter and launches opponents. Each connected strike restores 4 health and 12 stamina, capped at 100. Parries and solid cover still counter melee. Every strike carries the fighter in the aimed direction even without movement input, with one extra air lunge before landing and bounded horizontal boost. A brief unarmed lunge reduces incoming projectile damage and hit stun. Contact stays active during the forward 70% of a swing, so a lunge can connect after its first frame. Each opponent and piece of cover is hit at most once per strike, and healing is awarded once. Parries and strong interruptions cancel the active strike. Missing does not heal. A gap of 0.95 seconds or a heavy interruption resets the combo.
- Swept projectile collisions stop fast rounds at the first obstacle and prevent shots skipping thin cover.
- Sawblade and railgun hits cut a fighter into two separately simulated halves in one hit; a timed empty-handed parry still reflects the projectile. Map-mounted saws also kill on contact. Plasma and Tesla hits expose an illuminated skeleton; lethal hits leave it crumbling to ash. Ice creates a faceted shell that stops controls while leaving the body vulnerable to physics. A strong follow-up hit breaks the freeze for 50% extra damage; lethal follow-ups shatter into ice fragments. Swords cut on a lethal strike, fire leaves charred bodies and embers, and explosive kills scatter limbs.
- Minigun, heavy machine gun and shotgun hits bleed; lethal hits separate an arm and leg with physical motion. Ground spikes have solid bases and impale bodies at the contact point, holding them while blood drips and settles on surfaces. Destroying the spike releases the body. Blood is capped at 144 particles, fades within seven seconds and resets each round.
- Black holes affect a 620-unit radius for 5.5 seconds after a short warning. Strong inward and tangential forces lift grounded fighters and pull through cover; the 135-unit core stretches and consumes anyone, including the shooter. Bones become connected, curved particle strands. Platforms, furniture and trap machinery become deforming ribbons whose points orbit at different speeds and gradually spiral inward, entirely in 2D. Consumed pieces disappear; surviving twisted wreckage remains breakable with collision following its actual outline until the round ends. Guests and late joiners receive the same geometry. At most 60 pieces and 15 collision strips per ribbon bound simulation and network cost.
- Six fixed map-trap types: lethal flame geysers, powered conveyors, swinging spike balls, hydraulic crushers, moving saw rails and electrical traps. Theme-specific fixtures are visible before they activate and stay in place throughout the round. Vents, crushers and coils warn for one second, then cycle at unpredictable intervals. Geysers and crushers kill on contact; floors and cover shield other storeys from vents and electricity. Conveyors accelerate standing or prone fighters toward an edge. Breaking a mounting floor disables its trap. No random hazard zones spawn; traps stop in countdown and results.
- Continuous rounds with no winning score or match end. Each round win adds one point. The HUD marks the current leader or tied leaders. Scores persist across maps, but a slot starts at zero when a human replaces its AI or leaves and is replaced by AI.
- AI plans routes using the standing body, acceleration, single/double jumps, solid ceilings, furniture, moving lifts and spikes. Bots check takeoffs before jumping, remember failed routes and choose alternatives. They can wait on an elevator travelling toward their destination. Route rebuilding is spread across simulation ticks and begins during the countdown; existing routes remain available while new ones are prepared. Eliminated bots stop scheduling navigation work.
- Easy bots have delayed observations, imperfect aim and target leading, short firing bursts with rest periods, and fallible defensive reactions. Normal and Hard progressively improve these combat skills without changing health, damage or navigation.
- Bots prefer reachable targets, seek useful weapon upgrades, lead moving targets, maintain weapon-specific distance, account for recoil near actual edges rather than internal floor-panel seams, avoid firing explosives into nearby cover, and predict incoming projectiles before parrying or ducking. Unarmed, bat and sword bots commit to close fights or collect a reachable ranged weapon when the target is distant. Stalled floor-clearing attempts expire so bots can change position. Close-range shots are not vetoed by recoil caution. Heavy-gun bots deploy to fire and get up to relocate. They use the same controls, health, ammunition and physics as human players.
- Recoil and blast impulses persist for grounded, prone and airborne fighters. Movement counters them gradually. Heavy weapons kick harder; downward fire lifts the shooter, and rapid minigun fire can sustain upward movement with bounded speed. Only the deployed heavy machine gun is braced without kickback. Shotgun alternate fire consumes two shells for a double shot; plasma alternate fire consumes two rounds for a larger charged orb. Alternate and primary fire share a cooldown.
- Normal and nuclear grenades launch at 450 units/second with an upward toss and a 2.8-second fuse. Their unassisted horizontal travel stays below half the 2560-unit arena, including throws from high platforms; bounces shorten the range. The single-use nuclear grenade is thrown with attack or the throw-weapon action. Its blast has a 480-unit radius, 25% smaller than the previous radius; its 960-unit diameter spans 37.5% of the arena width. A fast circular flash and pressure front expose skeletons, which collapse into drifting ash. The blast cuts through structural floors and walls as well as breakable panels, consumes nearby props and ordnance, and leaves the background visible through the destroyed area. The initial incandescent fireball rises into rolling mushroom-cloud billows, cools to smoke and disperses. The cloud and drifting fallout fade completely within 12 seconds, leaving the background and permanent gap in the terrain. This aftermath is visual only. Terrain collision follows the circular cut to within 8 units. Cut elevator fragments stop moving; intact elevators and surviving props can still enter the cooled crater. Fighters inside the flash die, including the thrower; those outside it receive no nuclear damage or impulse. There are no secondary nuclear strikes or lingering radiation damage. Ordinary blast damage respects cover present at detonation, then its circular cut removes terrain within the blast radius. Both grenades detonate if they leave the arena. Scoring waits for any live nuclear grenade and its 3.2-second flash and ash sequence to resolve. Craters and altered terrain are included in online snapshots, including for late joiners.
- Every platform can be deformed by explosions, including structural floors, thick walls, stairs, wooden/glass panels and elevators. Rockets, grenades and plasma blasts carve circular holes; overlapping blasts excavate the surviving terrain. Ordinary bullets, rail/saw shots, fire and ice do not damage terrain. Unsupported fighters, furniture and loose weapons fall through the opening. Hit traps and spike strips are removed, and hit warped wreckage cannot recreate collision. Compact fragment identities and surviving spike geometry persist for existing guests and hot joiners. Terrain resets each round. Bots use explosives to open routes and take other routes when armed with ordinary guns.
- Shuffled or fixed arenas; sudden death after 120 seconds.
- Layered bass thuds and short impact flashes distinguish punches, kicks and the spinning finisher. Landed shots add minor screen shake; heavy fire and explosions have deeper reports. The nuke has a sustained bass drop and rumble. Audio uses compression and a final bounded output stage for overlapping impacts. Fighters have dark outlines, a local-player marker and health indicators. Pickup labels avoid fighters and each other; distant common labels are hidden while rare weapons remain identified. Lighting and room details are cached with the backdrop. Cached procedural cloud textures, bounded billow and ash counts and capped particles limit rendering work. Textures warm in idle time before combat. Reduced-motion preferences disable screen shake and replace the nuclear white flash with a subdued warm glow.

## Online architecture and prototype limits

The **game and assets are static files hosted on GitHub Pages**. The host's browser runs the authoritative 120 Hz simulation. Guests send only bounded controls at 30 Hz, receive state snapshots, and interpolate player skeletons, aim, moving platforms, falling hazards, projectiles, thrown weapons, blood, recovering ragdolls, curved body strands, deforming wreckage and moving effects from a short snapshot history. Hazard warnings and activation, cover health, debris, thrown weapons, melee poses, burning/freezing effects, black holes, chain lightning and projectiles are included in validated snapshots. Room discovery IDs remain stable while protocol 17 is negotiated explicitly; incompatible clients get an instruction to refresh both tabs. Network copies omit simulation-only fields and round fractional values to 0.01 world units; authoritative physics retains full precision. Moving entities have stable rendering IDs. Compatible browsers deflate snapshots once on the host and decode them on guests. Ordered binary transport supports chunking. Up to four unacknowledged snapshots per guest keep updates flowing across internet latency; cumulative acknowledgements and buffered-amount checks bound the queue. A stalled guest resumes with current state. Guests render against the host simulation clock with a short adaptive interpolation delay to absorb jitter. Canvas resolution follows the visible viewport, pickup glows and labels are cached, visual particles are capped, and unchanged HUD content is retained. Compression falls back to plain binary snapshots when unavailable. A guest cannot submit health, weapon, position or score changes. A trusted host can still modify the simulation: this is not an anti-cheat system.

The published game uses the Raspberry Pi's PeerJS signaling service and authenticated TURN relay. The host remains authoritative; direct WebRTC is used when possible, with the Pi relaying traffic when required. GitHub Pages serves only static assets. A disconnected signaling socket reconnects automatically without ending existing data channels. Joining has longer negotiation windows, one automatic retry, and separate errors for a host that did not answer, incompatible versions, and a host that answered but could not connect. Both sides load temporary TURN credentials before creating a connection, and long-running hosts refresh them for hot joins. A configured credential-service failure is reported instead of silently reverting to direct-only connections. Development builds without configured service URLs use public PeerJS signaling and STUN; direct connectivity depends on the networks involved. There is no voice/video capture, user account, analytics, or gameplay database.

**Quick match** searches eight fixed public tables using unique peer-ID claims. The first visitor becomes host; later visitors join that table. The host starts from the lobby with the selected slot modes. Joining players enter the lobby before start, or enter an available player slot if the game is running; rooms without open player slots are skipped. This is a small public queue, not region-aware or skill-based global matchmaking. AI slots are explicitly labelled in the scoreboard.

The host must keep the game open; it can run in a background tab. Joining or leaving affects only that slot, and other scores and the current round are preserved. A living fighter is taken over in place with its health and weapon; if an incoming player replaces an eliminated bot during combat, they spawn on a clear platform. Returning through the link is a new player and starts at zero. A stale disconnected peer cannot send controls into a reused slot. Host migration, rollback netcode, durable leaderboards are not implemented. Closing the host ends the room. The room service or relay may be unavailable; single player does not depend on it.

## Connection troubleshooting

Open **Connection details** from the host lobby or game menu and from the guest error screen after a failed join. Copy both reports. Reports retain negotiation states, offer/answer presence, generated candidate types, received candidate types, accepted remote candidate counts, selected candidate-pair types and numeric gathering errors. They omit IP addresses, SDP, room codes, profile data and credentials. Candidate gathering errors can occur for one address family or server even when another route succeeds; they alone do not establish a failed connection. The details observer does not replace PeerJS ICE/SDP handlers.

The last pre-hot-join revision (`8e8eb1b`) and the hot-join revision (`5d11d13`) both use unchanged PeerJS 1.5.5 defaults: Google STUN plus the bundled PeerJS TURN addresses. Revision `079ab4a` changes unordered JSON data to ordered binary data to support large snapshots; it does not modify ICE handlers. Revision `498a16a` uses explicit Google/Cloudflare STUN, removes the discontinued TURN endpoints, and retains the same PeerJS offer/answer/candidate implementation. Comparing two local browsers is not an external-network reproduction.

## Raspberry Pi room service and relay

The game supports a self-hosted PeerJS room service and authenticated coturn
relay on the Raspberry Pi. The host browser remains authoritative for the match.
See [the Pi deployment instructions](server/pi/README.md) for the isolated
services, router forwards, certificate renewal, validation and rollback.
`ROOM_SERVICE_URL` and `TURN_CREDENTIALS_URL` are public endpoint addresses in
GitHub Actions variables; the TURN signing secret stays on the Pi.

## Alternative internet relay setup

GitHub Pages serves the game but cannot run a TURN relay. A relay is necessary when the host and guest networks prevent a direct WebRTC connection. Refreshing the page or changing the lobby cannot solve that network restriction.

1. Create a [Metered Open Relay account](https://www.metered.ca/tools/openrelay/) and a TURN credential. The provider currently advertises a free monthly allowance; check the current limits before activating any paid service.
2. In this repository's Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → Variables, add `TURN_CREDENTIALS_URL`: the HTTPS **Get Credentials** URL for that individual TURN credential. Metered explicitly supports using its credential-scoped API key in frontend code. **Never use the account Secret Key.** The credential URL is public in the static build; it must only grant the intended client-level TURN access. Keep usage bounded in the provider dashboard and revoke the credential if necessary.
3. Run the **Test and publish Bonk Club** workflow again. It provides `VITE_TURN_CREDENTIALS_URL` at build time. Both the host and guest must refresh after deployment. Equivalent providers can supply an HTTPS endpoint returning an ICE-server array or `{ "iceServers": [...] }` with authenticated `turn:` / `turns:` entries.
4. Verify a real data channel with `iceTransportPolicy: "relay"`, confirm the selected ICE candidate is `relay`, and test an invite with the guest on a different internet connection. Local browser tests alone are not proof of this fix.

The credential endpoint is requested only when opening an online peer. Credentials are retained in memory for that room and are not saved in browser storage. Requests time out after eight seconds. A configured relay outage produces a specific error. Without a configured endpoint, direct networking remains available. A failed join is not automatically diagnosed as a missing relay.

## Development

Node.js 24 is used in CI.

```sh
npm ci
npm run dev
npm test
npm run build
```

`dist/` contains the deployable static game. Relative asset paths support a GitHub Pages repository subpath. Fonts are bundled locally. Offline play and direct connections need no keys. Internet relay configuration is described below. The GitHub Actions workflow tests, builds, and publishes changes on `main`.

Tests cover movement speed limits, preserved knockback, arena crossing time, climbing both outside routes through the taller rooms, long-range shots, extended round timing, all arena spawns, full elevator return trips with standing/prone passengers and loose weapons, automatic pickup, thrown-weapon damage and ammo retention, cover destruction, projectile occlusion and penetration, plasma bounces, blast shielding, input/state validation, combat, parries, round transitions, active-ragdoll constraints, prone collision, mouse aiming, four-peer room lifecycle, and disconnect handling. Touch tests cover simultaneous pointers, swipes, double-tap disambiguation, button activation, cancellation, physics integration, and portrait/landscape coordinate mapping. Transport tests with fake peers do not establish cross-network WebRTC reliability.

Trap tests cover persistent placement, warnings, lethal geysers, floor shielding, conveyor launches in both stances, moving collision bodies, crushers, destroyed mounts, phase changes and validated guest snapshots. Traversal regressions cover all spawn-to-spawn routes, Garden Houses bottom-floor fighters with four weapon types, melee pursuit and pistol upgrades, and the railgun stalemate on adjacent floor panels.

Tactical regression tests exercise wide ceilings at several heights, reachable target selection, weapon upgrades, projectile defence, furniture obstruction, shooting out an opponent's floor, falling furniture/weapons, blast destruction and round resets.

Combat and arsenal regressions cover chained melee with real movement, contact-only healing, parries and wall occlusion, bounded air lunges, bot melee commitment, standing recovery in both directions, grounded heavy-gun deployment, burn/chill expiry, ricochets and saw penetration, blocked lightning chains, homing and cluster shots, self-threatening black holes, projectile repulsion, rarity distribution including initial pickups, and validated snapshots for all weapons.

Connection tests cover explicit version rejection, compressed snapshots over the real binary encoding, acknowledgement backpressure and room-service reconnection. Weapon tests verify an opening nuke on all 24 maps and rare-weapon rotation across short rounds.

Slot regressions cover host-only configuration, occupied-slot removal, AI-only reservations, Player-only hot joins and departures, closed-slot persistence, and score resets. Render-state tests cover precision bounds, size reduction, stable entity identities, delivery jitter, stalls and round changes. Difficulty tests check delayed observations, burst/rest timing, accuracy differences and fallible defence while retaining the arena navigation and melee regressions.
